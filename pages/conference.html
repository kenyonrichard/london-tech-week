Conference Programme

Keynotes, breakout sessions, and deep-dive tracks across 7 stages — AI Arena, Core Stage, Transformation, Founders, Deep Tech, Ignition, and EQL:Lounge.

Day 1 — Visionaries Track

AMD, Mayor of London, Perplexity, OpenAI

The conference opened with a keynote speech from UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer, who used the podium to put smartphone developers on notice, instructing them to work on protecting children online. He also launched new initiatives and committed funding for AI in the UK. This was followed by Dr. Lisa Su (Chair & CEO, AMD) on building the compute foundation for the AI era. Sir Sadiq Khan joined Brent Hoberman CBE and Baroness Martha Lane Fox for a conversation on London's tech leadership.

Darren Hardman (Microsoft UK & Ireland) addressed the future of work, Aravind Srinivas (Perplexity) declared "AI is the Computer," and George Osborne (OpenAI) debated sovereign AI development in Europe alongside Minister Kanishka Narayan MP. The afternoon featured Ron Van Kemenade (Lloyds Banking Group), Stella Li (BYD), and a fireside on autonomous mobility with Uber and Wayve.

Day 2 — Applied AI & Enterprise

Wayve, Notion, Liz Kendall MP, Pat Gelsinger, GCHQ

Day 2 opened with Anne Keast-Butler (Director, GCHQ) on AI-driven approaches to national security, followed by Alex Kendall (Wayve) on embodied AI for autonomous vehicles and Ivan Zhao (Notion) on managing knowledge work in the AI age. The Rt Hon Liz Kendall MP, Secretary of State for Science, Innovation & Technology, delivered a keynote address reaffirming the government's commitment to UK AI leadership, outlining new investment in sovereign AI capabilities and AI skills programmes.

Pat Gelsinger (Former CEO, Intel & General Partner, Playground Global) joined a panel on backing the next generation of UK AI, emphasising the need for domestic hardware and compute infrastructure to reduce dependency on foreign supply chains. David Lammy MP, Foreign Secretary, addressed the geopolitical dimensions of AI governance, calling for international collaboration on AI safety standards and ethical frameworks. The afternoon featured parallel tracks across the Founders, Deep Tech, and Ignition stages covering enterprise resilience, marketing & sales, and capital & growth.

Day 3 — Enterprise Reinvented

LSEG, Deutsche Bank, Starling Bank, HRH The Prince of Wales

Day 3 closed the AI Arena's three-day run under an Enterprise Reinvented banner, chaired throughout by Katie Prescott (Technology Business Editor, The Times). Deloitte previewed its AI Workforce Survey, and CIOs from LSEG, Deutsche Bank, Booking.com and Starling Bank debated the real value of generative and agentic AI at enterprise scale. A public-sector panel — the DWP's Helen Wylie and the Ministry of Justice's Mark Thompson, moderated by the Tony Blair Institute's Alexander Iosad — asked whether automation can revitalise government services.

On the main AI Arena stage, HRH The Prince of Wales made his first appearance at London Tech Week, chairing a panel for his Homewards initiative alongside Jake Humphrey, Salesforce's Zahra Bahrololoumi, Bloomberg Associates' Linda Gibbs, and NatWest's Solange Chamberlain. The session launched the UK's first Homelessness Data Lab, built with LandAid and Salesforce, pooling data across more than 25 partners — including local authorities and the Ministry of Housing, Communities & Local Government — to spot people at risk of losing their home earlier, tested first across six pilot areas. As William put it: homelessness is "not inevitable... it is predictable, and therefore preventable."
